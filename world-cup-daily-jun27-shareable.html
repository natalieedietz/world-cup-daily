The Group Stage Signs Off With Messi, Ronaldo, England, and a Forty-Four-Year-Old Grudge That Has Been Quietly Simmering Since Before Most of These Players Were Born

Your daily World Cup briefing

The last day of the group stage, and the schedule has saved its richest card for the encore: six matches in simultaneous pairs, Messi and Ronaldo on the same afternoon, England chasing top spot while looking faintly nervous about it, and, gloriously, Algeria versus Austria, a fixture dragging the ghost of the 1982 Disgrace of Gijón blinking back into the daylight. First, though, raise a glass to Cabo Verde, because yesterday an island of 525,000 people did something no nation that small has ever done, and it deserves a standing ovation before we get to the chaos.

The Grudge Match ⚽ Aït-Nouri · Man City ⚽ Sabitzer · Dortmund

Algeria v Austria

A winner-takes-second decider soaked in forty-four years of history. Algeria have waited since 1982 to settle a score with Austria, and tonight, at last, they get their rematch.

Algeria 38% Draw 30% Austria 32%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Some fixtures carry history. This one carries a grudge with its own Wikipedia page. At the 1982 World Cup, West Germany and Austria contrived the infamous "Disgrace of Gijón," a bloodless 1-0 in which, once West Germany scored early, both sides simply stopped trying, a result that sent the two European nations through and eliminated Algeria, who had already played their final game. The outrage was so total that FIFA changed the rules forever, mandating that the final group games kick off simultaneously. Algeria have been waiting forty-four years to look Austria in the eye with something on the line. Tonight they get it.

The stakes are real beyond the symbolism. Argentina have won the group, so this is a straight shoot-out for second place: Austria, with the superior goal difference, need only a draw to go through, while Algeria must win. There is even a deliciously perverse subplot, because the runner-up is likely to draw Spain in the round of 32, meaning second place is a prize some might secretly prefer to avoid. But do not insult Algeria by suggesting they would calculate. As their federation has pointedly noted, Algeria have never played for anything but victory, and they intend to win this one outright, history and Spain be damned.

On the pitch, it is finely poised. Algeria carry real quality through Manchester City's Rayan Aït-Nouri and the veteran guile of Riyad Mahrez, while Austria are Ralf Rangnick's disciplined, well-drilled unit, content to sit on a draw they would happily take. The football may or may not be a classic. The narrative is unmissable. Forty-four years of grievance, ninety minutes to settle it. Do not even think about changing the channel.

Upset Watch

Genuinely even, which only adds to the tension. Austria's draw-is-enough position could tempt them into a cautious shell, exactly the kind of passivity that invites a motivated Algeria to pounce. With this much history in the air, a flashpoint or a late winner feels almost scripted.

England, loaded and already qualified, against an eliminated Panama side playing for pride. The Three Lions need a win to top the group and avoid the dreaded scenic route.

England arrive in New Jersey in the curious position of being both excellent and faintly unconvincing, which is the most England thing about them. They opened with a thumping 4-2 win over Croatia, then drew a blank in a goalless stalemate with Ghana that launched the customary fortnight of national soul-searching. They are already through, but they need a win here to top Group L and steer clear of a nastier knockout path. The squad is absurdly deep, Kane, Bellingham, Saka, Foden, Palmer, and on paper this should be straightforward.

Panama, sadly, are already eliminated, their tournament ended by that agonizing last-gasp loss to Ghana and a narrow defeat to Croatia. They will play for pride, for their traveling support, and for the small glory of giving a giant a fright on the way out. Los Canaleros are organized and physical, and a team with nothing to lose can be awkward for a side carrying the weight of expectation, but the gulf in quality is enormous and England should have far too much.

Realistically this is a procession, and the only suspense is the margin and whether England can rediscover the attacking fluency that deserted them against Ghana. For Thomas Tuchel, it is about momentum, sharpness and topping the group cleanly. For Panama, it is about leaving the World Cup with their heads high and perhaps one moment worth taking home. England by a comfortable few, you would think, but this is England, so nobody is allowed to fully relax.

Survival Decider ⚽ Gvardiol · Man City ⚽ Kudus · Tottenham

Croatia v Ghana

The 2018 finalists against the Black Stars, with second place and survival on the line. Ghana are already through; Croatia must avoid defeat or risk going home.

Croatia 40% Draw 30% Ghana 30%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Croatia's tournament has been a slow-motion lesson in mortality. The 2018 finalists and 2022 bronze medallists were hammered 4-2 by England, then recovered to edge Panama 1-0, and now arrive at a final game where the math is unforgiving: avoid defeat against Ghana and they progress, most likely as a third-placed team, but lose and Luka Modrić's glittering international career could end on the spot. For an ageing side built around a 40-year-old midfield maestro, every minute now feels borrowed.

Ghana, by contrast, are sitting pretty. The Black Stars have already qualified on four points, having beaten Panama and held England to that disciplined goalless draw, and they carry a genuine match-winner in Tottenham's Mohammed Kudus. A win lifts them above whoever they need to and could even leapfrog England for a better seeding. Crucially, they can play this with freedom while Croatia play it with fear, and freedom tends to be the better fuel in a tense knockout-style game.

Croatia are slight favorites on quality, and Modrić and Mateo Kovačić can still control a game when they dictate its rhythm. But Ghana have the pace, the physicality and the Kudus-shaped threat to punish a creaking Croatian back line, and the pressure sits squarely on the Europeans. If Ghana score first, the sight of Croatia chasing the game with their tournament life on the line could become the drama of the afternoon. A proud old side staring at the exit makes for compelling, nervy viewing.

Upset Watch

Very live. Croatia looked old and stretched against England, and Ghana have the tools to exploit it. A Ghana win would send a former finalist crashing out and might just write the final chapter of Luka Modrić's international story. The tension will be exquisite.

Argentina arrive at the final group game with everything already settled. The big tactical question is simply how much Lionel Scaloni chooses to rest, weighing a buzzing crowd's desire to see the great man against the sense in keeping precious legs fresh for the knockouts. Jordan, at their first-ever World Cup, are already eliminated, and their tournament has been a story of brave defeats: a respectable loss to Austria, a heartbreaking late collapse against Algeria. Whether he plays ninety minutes or twenty, the cameras will follow his every touch, because greatness on a farewell tour is its own reward. Tune in for him, and stay for the inevitable standing ovation.

Group K · 4:30 PM PT · Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta

Win-or-Bust ⚽ Wissa · Newcastle

DR Congo v Uzbekistan

A straight knockout in all but name. The winner keeps alive real hopes of a third-place berth; the loser goes home. Two debutants-at-heart with everything to play for.

DR Congo 47% Draw 28% Uzbekistan 25%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

While the glamor games hog the spotlight, this quietly is one of the most consequential matches of the day for the teams involved. Both DR Congo and Uzbekistan still harbor genuine hopes of sneaking into the knockouts as one of the eight best third-placed sides, but the equation is brutally simple: win, and you give yourself a real chance; anything less and you are almost certainly going home. It is a final-day knockout dressed in group-stage clothing.

DR Congo are the marginal favorites and have the better of the recent narrative, having opened the tournament by holding Ronaldo's Portugal to a 1-1 draw through sheer organisation and grit before a narrow loss to Colombia. Back at a World Cup after a 52-year absence, the Leopards are physical, disciplined and carry Premier League quality in Newcastle's Yoane Wissa. A win likely sends them through, and they will fancy it against a Uzbek side with a battered goal difference to repair.

Uzbekistan, at their first-ever World Cup under Fabio Cannavaro, have been brave but face the steeper climb: they likely need not just a win but a sizeable one to drag their minus-seven goal difference back toward respectability. That is a tall order against an organized DR Congo, but desperation breeds adventure, and a Uzbek side throwing caution to the wind could make this a genuinely open, end-to-end affair. For two nations savouring rare World Cup air, it is everything.

Upset Watch

Hard to frame as an upset when it is this even, but Uzbekistan must win big, which means taking risks that could leave them exposed to the exact counterattacking threat DR Congo thrive on. The team that handles the pressure of a winner-takes-all night better will likely prevail.

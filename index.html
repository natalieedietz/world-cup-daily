First, What You Missed · Friday 3 July

The Round of 32 signed off with a fairytale that nearly came true, a Salah panenka, and a Colombian stroll.

Fairytale, So Close
Round of 32 · Miami · after extra time
ARG Argentina 3–2 Cabo Verde CPV
The smallest nation ever in the knockouts pushed the champions to the brink.

Cabo Verde, an archipelago of half a million people, walked into Miami and very nearly mugged the defending world champions in front of Messi's adopted home crowd. Lionel Messi opened the scoring, but the Blue Sharks refused to read the script: Deroy Duarte equalized, then after Lisandro Martínez restored Argentina's lead in extra time, Sidny Lopes Cabral leveled it again in the 103rd minute. Cue mild panic across Argentina. In the end it took a late Cabo Verde own goal to separate them, a cruel way for a fairytale to end, but end it did, 3-2 to the champions. Cabo Verde go home as legends who scared the living daylights out of the best team on the planet. Take a bow, Blue Sharks.

Panenka Merchant Round of 32 · Arlington · pens
EGY Egypt1–1Australia AUS

Egypt reached the last 16 for the first time in their history, and did it with maximum style. After Australia's Mohamed Hany became the first player ever to score two own goals in a single World Cup (a record nobody dreams of), the game went to penalties, where Mohamed Salah casually chipped a panenka down the middle because of course he did. Egypt won the shootout 4-2 and now face Argentina. The Socceroos, agonizingly, still await a first-ever knockout win.

Round of 32 · Kansas City
COL Colombia1–0Ghana GHA

Colombia did exactly what quietly-good teams do: won without fuss. Jhon Arias struck in the 14th minute and Los Cafeteros controlled the rest, dominating possession and peppering the Ghana goal while the Black Stars managed zero shots on target all night. It finished 1-0, flattering Ghana if anything, and Colombia march into a last-16 date with Switzerland looking every bit like a team that fancies a deep run. Efficient, composed, and quietly dangerous.

The Storylines

A Co-Host's Fourth of July

There is a lovely wrinkle to today: on Independence Day, the co-host still standing is Canada, quietly having the best World Cup in their history. They reached the last 16 with a stoppage-time winner over South Africa, Alphonso Davies is finally expected to start after injury, and Jesse Marsch has cheerfully branded the Morocco game a "free hit." No pressure, everything to gain, a whole continent half-watching. If the Canadians spring a shock, it will be the story of the tournament so far. Northern neighbors, we salute you.

Club Connections

Familiar faces everywhere. Canada field Alphonso Davies (Bayern Munich) and Jonathan David (Juventus); Morocco counter with Achraf Hakimi (PSG) and Brahim Díaz (Real Madrid). France, as ever, are absurd: Kylian Mbappé (Real Madrid), Ousmane Dembélé (Barcelona) and Michael Olise (Bayern). Paraguay's resistance is anchored by the veteran forward Antonio Sanabria and a back line that treats clean sheets as a religion.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

Only two games, both in prime West Coast hours, so you can watch soccer and still make your barbecue. The 10am Canada v Morocco is the one with real jeopardy and a feel-good angle, a co-host underdog against a genuine contender. The 2pm France v Paraguay is more about whether the French juggernaut gets frustrated by the tournament's most stubborn defense. Grill in one hand, phone in the other. Fire up the group chat.

Giant-Killer Watch · Paraguay

Do not sleep on Paraguay. Yes, France are enormous favorites, but Paraguay are the team that already sent four-time champions Germany home, grinding out a goalless slog and winning the shootout. They defend like their lives depend on it, they slow games to a crawl, and they have zero interest in playing the entertaining, open football France craves. If anyone can turn a glamor tie into a nervy, ninety-minute headache, it is these guys. Underdog hearts, take note.

Canada v Morocco

On paper this is a mismatch, and the paper has a point. Morocco are the real deal: the first African nation to reach a World Cup semifinal, back in 2022, and they arrive on a genuinely absurd 33-game unbeaten run, having already dumped the Netherlands out of this tournament on penalties. They are disciplined, deep, and dripping with quality, Achraf Hakimi marauding from full-back, Brahim Díaz pulling strings, and a defensive structure that strangled the Dutch. They will dominate the ball, and they will fully expect to win. The bookmakers make them clear favorites for good reason.

And yet Canada have earned their moment, and they will not be here to make up the numbers. The co-hosts reached the last 16 with a dramatic stoppage-time winner over South Africa, they have registered more shots on target than any team in the tournament (their only problem has been finishing them), and crucially, Alphonso Davies, their turbocharged Bayern Munich talisman, is finally expected to start after a tournament spent nursing his way back to fitness. Jonathan David gives them a genuine goal threat, and a Fourth of July crowd in Houston will roar them on. Jesse Marsch calling it a "free hit" is exactly the loose, fearless mindset that springs upsets.

The likeliest outcome is a Morocco win; they are simply the better, more experienced side, and their knockout nous is proven. But knockout football does not read rankings, Canada have the pace and the belief to make this uncomfortable, and if Davies is fit and firing, they carry a real threat on the counter. If the co-hosts take an early lead and make Morocco anxious, Houston could witness something special. Expect Morocco to advance, but do not be shocked if Canada make them sweat every step of the way. The free hit has teeth.

Upset Watch

Live and delicious. Canada are the tournament's leaders in shots on target, and with a fit Alphonso Davies they have genuine game-breaking pace. If they finally convert their chances and the home crowd lifts them, Morocco's unbeaten run could meet a Fourth of July ambush. It would be the shock of the knockouts so far.

France look like a team on a mission. They have won every game they have played, dispatched Sweden 3-0 in the last round, and boast an attack so deep it is almost unfair: Kylian Mbappé, the Ballon d'Or holder Ousmane Dembélé, and Michael Olise, all in ominous form, behind a squad that Didier Deschamps can rotate without dropping a decibel of quality. They are the clear tournament favorites, and against most opponents the only question would be the final margin. Mbappé, still three goals shy of Messi's all-time World Cup record, will fancy adding to his tally too.

But Paraguay are not most opponents, and they will not care one bit that France are prettier. This is the side that already knocked out four-time world champions Germany, and they did it the only way they know how: by defending in a low, disciplined block, slowing the game to a maddening crawl, fouling cleverly, and dragging a superior team into the kind of grinding, joyless slog that eventually went to penalties. They have no interest in an open, flowing game, and every interest in making France frustrated, anxious, and eventually desperate. It is deeply unglamorous, and it works.

The overwhelming likelihood is that France's sheer quality tells, and that they eventually pick the lock and win with something to spare, especially if they score early and force Paraguay out of their shell. But if the first goal does not come, and Paraguay's bus stays parked, the tension will build, the French crowd will fret, and the ghosts of "we already did this to Germany" will start to whisper. Expect France to advance, expect them to have to be patient, and do not assume it will be the stroll the odds suggest. Fortresses are boring right up until they hold.

Upset Watch

Statistically slim, but Paraguay have already authored one giant-killing this tournament, so nobody can call it impossible. Their entire game plan is to make elite teams miserable, and if they keep it goalless deep into the second half, the pressure shifts entirely onto the favorites. France are far better, but Germany were favorites too.

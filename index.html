Germany Trip Over the Exact Banana Skin We Flagged, the USMNT Lose a Game That Did Not Count in the Most USMNT Way Possible, and Haaland Meets Mbappé in a Heavyweight Friday

Briefing for Natalie

Groups G, H and I close today, six matches in simultaneous pairs, and the headline act is a genuine collision of titans: Erling Haaland's Norway against Kylian Mbappé's France, both already through, slugging it out purely for top spot and bragging rights. There is also Spanish jeopardy, a Belgian existential crisis, and an African scramble. But first, we must process yesterday, when Germany did the precise thing we predicted, the USA lost a match designed to be meaningless, and Curaçao's beautiful dream finally ran out of road.

First, What You Missed · Thursday 25 June

We called the German banana skin. We did not need to call the USMNT chaos; that one calls itself.

Told You So Group E · East Rutherford
🇪🇨 Ecuador2–1🇩🇪 Germany

Yesterday this very briefing described Ecuador v Germany as "the banana-skin slot of the day, the heavily favored giant with nothing to play for against the hungry side that needs everything." Reader, the giant located the banana skin, stepped on it with both feet, and went down in a heap. Ecuador, backs to the wall, conjured the upset of the round to stun an already-qualified Germany 2-1. Nagelsmann's side were through regardless, but a four-time world champion losing to anyone is a four-time world champion losing, and the rest of the draw took careful notes.

Gloriously Pointless Group D · Inglewood (SoFi)
🇹🇷 Türkiye3–2🇺🇸 USA

The USMNT had already won the group, so Pochettino made nine changes, handed seven players their first World Cup start, and let SoFi enjoy a stress-free night. The squad players promptly conspired to lose 3-2 to a 98th-minute Türkiye winner, in a game with precisely zero stakes, in front of 70,000 people, which is somehow the most on-brand thing imaginable. Pulisic came off the bench for a cameo. The USA still top the group and still march into the round of 32. Nothing was lost except the match. File under: fine, actually, probably, we think.

Group D · Santa Clara
🇵🇾 Paraguay0–0🇦🇺 Australia

The actual Group D drama next door was a tense, cagey, deeply tactical 0-0 in which both teams correctly worked out that a draw probably sent them both through, and then defended like their lives depended on it. Australia took second on goal difference; Paraguay finished third and now sweat on the best-third-place math, which is the World Cup equivalent of waiting for exam results you are fairly sure you passed. Not a classic. Entirely effective.

Fairytale's End Group E · Philadelphia
🇨🇼 Curaçao0–2🇨🇮 Ivory Coast

And so the great Curaçao adventure reaches its final page. The smallest nation ever to play a World Cup, the island of 156,000 whose goalkeeper made fifteen saves and whose draw with Ecuador melted hearts worldwide, lost 2-0 to a sharper, hungrier Ivory Coast and bowed out. No shame, only applause. They came as a footnote and leave as one of the stories of the tournament. Willemstad, take a bow. We will not forget you, and neither will the record books.

Heavyweight Bout ⚽ Haaland · Man City ⚽ Mbappé · Real Madrid

Norway v France

Two of the deadliest forwards alive, two teams already safely through, one top spot worth fighting for. This is a prizefight with nothing and everything on the line.

Norway 30% Draw 28% France 42%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Sometimes the schedule hands you a gift. Both Norway and France have already booked their place in the knockout rounds, which on a lesser day might mean two rotated sides going through the motions. Instead it means two of the most fearsome attacking teams on earth, freed from the fear of elimination, swinging for top spot and the smoother knockout path that comes with it. A draw sends France through first on goal difference, so Norway must actually win, which is precisely the spice this needs.

France are favorites and look every inch a tournament contender, Kylian Mbappé already past Messi on the all-time World Cup scoring chart, Ousmane Dembélé and Michael Olise providing the supporting fireworks, and a squad so deep that Didier Deschamps can rest stars as a luxury rather than a gamble. They have barely moved out of second gear and still beaten everyone in front of them. This is a side that wins these games more often than not, and they know it.

And yet Norway have the one man who can settle any match in a single heartbeat. Erling Haaland already has four goals this tournament and is operating at the peak of his terrifying powers, with Arsenal's Martin Ødegaard threading the passes. Norway are at their first World Cup since 1998 and playing without fear. If this becomes the shoot-out it threatens to be, Haaland versus the French defense is the single most appetising individual duel of the entire group stage. Do not miss a minute.

Upset Watch

Not an upset so much as a coin flip with elite finishers. France are favored, but Norway must win and have the firepower to do it. If Haaland gets two clean chances, the holders of top spot could change hands in the time it takes to blink.

Win-Big-or-Bust ⚽ Sarr · Crystal Palace

Senegal v Iraq

Two sides who must not only win but win big, chasing a positive goal difference and a slender third-place lifeline. Desperation makes for entertaining football.

Senegal 68% Draw 19% Iraq 13%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Group I has been brutal to its bottom two. Both Senegal and Iraq sit pointless, both have been outclassed by France and Norway, and both now arrive at a final game that is technically still alive and realistically hanging by a thread. To have any hope of sneaking through as one of the eight best third-placed teams, they likely need not just a win but a big one, enough to drag a battered goal difference back toward respectability. It is a long shot dressed as a lifeline.

Senegal are the stronger side and should win this comfortably. One of Africa's heavyweights, they reached the last 16 in 2022 and carry genuine quality through Crystal Palace's Ismaïla Sarr, who struck twice late against Norway, and a squad with real pedigree even in what is likely Sadio Mané's final World Cup. The talent gap is significant, and against an Iraq side that has shipped goals freely, Senegal have every reason to chase the emphatic scoreline their situation demands.

Iraq, at only their second World Cup and first since 1986, have been brave but outgunned, and the dream is all but mathematically over. Still, there is pride to play for and the faint romance of a famous goal or two to take home. Realistically Senegal win, perhaps handsomely, and then join everyone else in refreshing the third-place permutations. For Iraq, it is about leaving a mark before the curtain falls.

Upset Watch

An Iraq win would be a real shock, but the more meaningful drama is the margin. Senegal need goals, plural, and if Iraq dig in and frustrate them, Senegal's own qualification hopes shrink with every minute the scoreline stays tight.

Group H · 5:00 PM PT · Estadio Akron, Guadalajara

Champions Under Pressure ⚽ Pedri · Barcelona ⚽ Valverde · Real Madrid

Uruguay v Spain

The European champions need to avoid defeat to be safe. Bielsa's Uruguay must beat them to survive. A heavyweight with a knockout berth riding on it.

Uruguay 22% Draw 28% Spain 50%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Spain have rediscovered themselves at exactly the right moment. After that mortifying goalless draw with Cabo Verde left them briefly bottom of the group and the subject of a thousand panicked think-pieces, they restored order by hammering Saudi Arabia 4-0 with Lamine Yamal back to full sharpness. They need only avoid defeat here to secure their place in the knockouts, and with a win they top the group. The talent is overwhelming, Pedri and Rodri running midfield, Yamal the teenage x-factor, and La Roja look like contenders again.

Uruguay, though, are wounded and dangerous, which is the worst combination to face. Marcelo Bielsa's side have underwhelmed, drawing with Saudi Arabia and then being held by Cabo Verde, and they now find themselves needing a win against the European champions simply to guarantee a knockout spot. Built around the relentless Federico Valverde and the unpredictable Darwin Núñez, Uruguay have the quality and the desperation to make this a genuine battle, and Bielsa sides are never short on intensity.

Spain are favorites and should have enough, but a draw suits them and not Uruguay, which means La Celeste must come out swinging and take risks a more comfortable side would not. That tilts the game toward chaos, and chaos is where upsets are born. For the neutral, it is a delicious collision: Spanish silk against Uruguayan steel, with one of these proud footballing nations potentially heading for a shock early exit.

Upset Watch

Real and spicy. Uruguay must win and will throw everything forward, and Spain were held by Cabo Verde not long ago. If Núñez has one of his unplayable nights and Bielsa's press bites, the champions could be in for a deeply uncomfortable evening in Guadalajara.

Group H · 5:00 PM PT · NRG Stadium, Houston

Island Dream ⚽ Vozinha

Cabo Verde v Saudi Arabia

The 525,000-strong island nation can reach the knockout rounds with a win. Saudi Arabia must win to keep their own flickering hopes alive. History beckons in Houston.

Cabo Verde 42% Draw 30% Saudi Arabia 28%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Pinch yourself, because this is real. Cabo Verde, an archipelago of roughly 525,000 people at their first-ever World Cup, can reach the knockout rounds of the planet's biggest sporting event with a win this evening. They are not here to make up the numbers, either: they held the European champions Spain to a goalless draw and then drew 2-2 with two-time winners Uruguay, with veteran goalkeeper Vozinha becoming a global folk hero in the process. A nation that had never before set foot at a World Cup is ninety minutes from the last 32.

Saudi Arabia stand in their way, and the Green Falcons have their own reasons to win. After holding Uruguay and then being thumped 4-0 by Spain, they must take all three points and hope results elsewhere break their way to have any chance of progressing. They have the experience and the individual quality, with Salem Al-Dawsari capable of moments of magic, to make this difficult, and a team fighting for its own survival is rarely a comfortable opponent.

Cabo Verde are slight favorites and, remarkably, control their own destiny: win, and they are through, no calculators required. The romance is almost overwhelming, a tiny island side on the brink of a feat that would dwarf even their group-stage heroics. Expect Houston to adopt them wholesale, expect Vozinha to be busy, and expect one of the most joyous scenes of the tournament if they hold their nerve. Adopt them and believe.

Upset Watch

The delicious twist is that the "upset," a microstate reaching the knockouts, is actually the favored outcome here. The genuine danger is a desperate Saudi Arabia side that must win and could punish any Cabo Verde nerves. Tension guaranteed.

Group G · 8:00 PM PT · BC Place, Vancouver

Belgian Nerves ⚽ De Bruyne · Napoli

New Zealand v Belgium

Belgium's golden generation, still allergic to convincing anyone, against a New Zealand side chasing the first World Cup win in their history. A win sends Belgium through.

New Zealand 9% Draw 15% Belgium 76%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Belgium have spent this tournament doing their very favorite thing: looking enormously talented and achieving almost nothing with it. Two draws from two games, against Egypt and Iran, mean the golden generation arrives in Vancouver needing a win to be sure of progressing as a top-two side, lest they slip into the third-place lottery or worse. Kevin De Bruyne can still bend a game to his will, Romelu Lukaku still leads the line, but the nagging sense of a closing window grows louder with every uninspiring ninety minutes.

New Zealand, by glorious contrast, are chasing history of their own. The All Whites have never won a single World Cup match across their entire existence, drawing their way through 1982, 2010 and the early part of this campaign with admirable stubbornness. Captain Chris Wood remains a genuine aerial threat, and a spirited, physical New Zealand side would love nothing more than to author their first-ever victory at the expense of a big European name on the grandest stage.

Belgium are heavy favorites and really should win this, given the gulf in quality, and a victory settles their business cleanly. But this is Belgium, a team that has turned the act of overcomplicating simple games into an art form, and New Zealand are precisely the sort of organized, awkward opponent capable of frustrating them into another anxious night. If the All Whites score first, Vancouver could become very interesting very quickly.

Upset Watch

Statistically slim, but Belgium have made a habit of nervy, underwhelming performances, and New Zealand are chasing a historic first win with nothing to lose. A draw would suit the All Whites enormously and pile real pressure on a fragile-looking Belgium.

Group G · 8:00 PM PT · Lumen Field, Seattle

Nightcap Decider ⚽ Salah · Liverpool

Egypt v Iran

Mohamed Salah's likely final World Cup hangs in the balance. Egypt lead the group and can seal it; Iran must match Belgium's result to survive. A proper last-night thriller.

Egypt 42% Draw 30% Iran 28%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

The nightcap is a proper final-day knot. Egypt top Group G on four points and will qualify for the knockouts if they simply avoid defeat, with a win guaranteeing top spot. They lean, as ever, heavily on Mohamed Salah, at 34 and almost certainly playing his last World Cup, chasing the knockout berth that has eluded Egypt for decades. Around him sits enough quality to control a game, and the Pharaohs know that ninety composed minutes likely sends them through.

Iran, by contrast, must win, or at least match Belgium's result against New Zealand, and even then their fate may rest on goal-difference fine print. Drawn level on points with Belgium after two stubborn, disciplined performances, they are exactly the kind of physical, well-organized side that can drag a more talented opponent into a scrap. They will press, they will frustrate, and they will fancy their chances of catching an Egypt side that has more to lose by overcommitting.

Egypt are marginal favorites and have the better player on the pitch, but a draw could yet undo Iran while suiting Egypt, which sets up a tense, tactical, nerve-jangling occasion with knockout spots changing on the scoreboard next door. For the neutral, the pull is simple and irresistible: Salah, one of the Premier League's all-time greats, fighting to extend a glittering career by one more World Cup round. Stay up for it.

Upset Watch

Live and tangled. Iran must win and have the discipline to make it awkward, and if Salah is well marshalled, Egypt can look ordinary. With both Group G games kicking off together, a single goal in Seattle or Vancouver could flip the entire qualifying picture.

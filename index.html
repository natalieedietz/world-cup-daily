First, What You Missed · Sunday 5 July

England exorcised a forty-year-old demon with ten men, and Haaland sent the five-time champions home.

Instant Classic
Round of 16 · Mexico City (Azteca)
ENG England 3–2 Mexico MEX
Ten men for the final 36 minutes. The ghost of 1986 is finally buried.

Well. Nobody saw that coming from this England. Jude Bellingham scored twice in the space of 98 seconds late in the first half to stun a rocking Azteca, the first player to score two goals there in a World Cup since some fellow named Maradona in 1986. Julián Quiñones pulled one back before the break, Harry Kane restored the two-goal cushion from the spot on the hour, and then everything caught fire: Jarell Quansah was sent off after a VAR review in the 54th minute, Raúl Jiménez converted a Mexico penalty, and England spent the final half-hour-plus defending like their lives depended on it with ten men, at altitude, in the most hostile building in football. They held. In the exact stadium where the Hand of God broke their hearts forty years ago, England buried the ghost. Pickford was superhuman, Bellingham was magnificent, and the neutrals are, frankly, stunned.

Champions Out Round of 16 · East Rutherford

The other game was no small thing either: Norway knocked out Brazil. Erling Haaland, who apparently does not care that the opposition are five-time world champions, bagged a brace to send the Seleção packing and fire Norway into the first World Cup quarterfinal in their history. Brazil huffed and puffed and pulled one back, but the big man would not be denied. Somewhere, an entire nation of Norwegians is losing its mind. Haaland now gets England on Saturday in Miami. Buckle up.

The Storylines

The World Cup Wants to Visit Us Again

Here is the local angle, and it is a big one. If the USA beat Belgium tonight, their quarterfinal will be played this Saturday at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, on your actual doorstep. That is right: win one game in Seattle, and the World Cup comes back to our backyard with the home nation in it. The last time the tournament was at SoFi, Spain gave a clinic and Yamal scored in his hometown. Now the USMNT have a chance to bring the whole circus back for a last-eight blockbuster. Root accordingly.

Club Connections

Stacked, as you would expect. Spain bring Lamine Yamal (Barcelona) and Pedri (Barcelona); Portugal answer with Cristiano Ronaldo (Al-Nassr) and Bruno Fernandes (Man Utd). In Seattle, the USA lean on Christian Pulišić (AC Milan) and Weston McKennie (Juventus), while Belgium counter with the peerless Kevin De Bruyne (Napoli), goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois (Real Madrid) and the ever-dangerous Romelu Lukaku.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

Both games are in prime West Coast hours, which is frankly considerate of them. The 12pm Spain v Portugal is a genuine heavyweight collision and probably the best pure-football watch of the round, an Iberian civil war stuffed with world-class talent. But the 5pm USA v Belgium is the one to gather people for, a nail-biting coin flip with a home nation, a raucous Seattle crowd, and a SoFi quarterfinal dangling as the prize. Fire up the group chat and lay in snacks.

The Last Dance, Again · Ronaldo

Cristiano Ronaldo, 41 years old and still allergic to the concept of retirement, finally scored his first-ever World Cup knockout goal for Portugal in the last round, a penalty against Croatia after roughly two decades of trying. Now he gets Spain, the old enemy, in what is surely one of the final major matches of his staggering career. Whatever your feelings about the man, watching him try to topple the tournament favorites one more time is appointment viewing. Legends do not fade quietly, and he never has.

This is the one the purists circled the moment the bracket dropped. Spain came into this tournament as many people's favorites, and it is not hard to see why: a dizzying carousel of possession, the generational teenager Lamine Yamal on one flank, Pedri conducting midfield like he has the game on slow motion, and Mikel Oyarzabal in form after a two-goal afternoon against Austria. They control games so thoroughly that opponents can go long stretches barely touching the ball, and when they are at full tilt they are close to unplayable. La Roja are the standard everyone else is being measured against.

Portugal, though, are exactly the sort of loaded, unpredictable opponent who can ruin a favorite's day, and they will relish being underestimated. Their squad is absurdly deep, Bruno Fernandes, Vitinha, Bernardo Silva, Rafael Leão, and of course the 41-year-old comet himself, Cristiano Ronaldo, who finally broke his World Cup knockout duck with a penalty against Croatia and will fancy tormenting the old enemy one last time. Portugal can be maddeningly inconsistent, capable of looking both like champions and like strangers who just met, but on their best day they have every tool required to knock Spain out. The talent is not the question. The cohesion is.

Expect a tense, high-quality, occasionally spicy affair between two neighbors who know each other far too well. Spain are rightly favored, and their control should eventually tell, but this is the kind of derby where form and logic sometimes take the night off, and where one moment of Ronaldo defiance or Yamal magic could settle everything. Whoever survives lands a favorable-looking route deeper into the tournament. Do not miss a minute, and do not assume the favorites will stroll. Iberian derbies rarely read the script.

Upset Watch

Genuine. Portugal are underdogs only by the slimmest margin, and they have the individual quality to hurt anyone. If they can disrupt Spain's rhythm and turn it into a broken, transitional game, Leão's pace and Ronaldo's nose for a moment could swing it. Spain are the better side, but "better side" has been a flimsy shield this tournament.

This is the big one for us, and the bookmakers cannot separate them, which is exactly how a home-nation knockout tie should feel. The USA have been quietly excellent, scoring two or more goals in all four of their matches and reaching the last 16 with a gritty win over Bosnia. And there is very good news on the team sheet: leading scorer Folarin Balogun, who many assumed would be suspended after his red card against Bosnia, has been cleared to play after his ban was set aside, restoring the focal point of Pochettino's attack. Add Christian Pulišić pulling the strings and a Seattle crowd that will be absolutely deafening, and the co-hosts have every reason to believe.

Belgium, as ever, are a puzzle wrapped in world-class talent. Kevin De Bruyne remains one of the finest passers the sport has ever produced, Thibaut Courtois is a wall in goal, and Romelu Lukaku is a menace off the bench, but this Belgian side also carries a leaky defense and a habit of making life unnecessarily difficult for itself. They needed a preposterous late comeback from two goals down to escape Senegal in the last round, so they are battle-tested, but also eminently beatable if the USA can get at that back line. They will have more possession and more polish; whether they have more resolve is the question.

This genuinely is a coin flip, and the atmosphere in Seattle plus the SoFi carrot make it the must-watch game of the day for anyone in Los Angeles. The USA have the pace, the crowd, and now their main striker back; Belgium have the individual class to win any game in a single De Bruyne moment. Expect an open, nervy, end-to-end tie that could easily go to extra time or penalties, and know that the stakes could not be more local: win this, and the World Cup comes to Inglewood on Saturday. Gather everyone. This is the one.

Upset Watch

Barely an upset in either direction, that is the beauty of it. Belgium are technically superior on paper, but the USA have home advantage, a ferocious crowd, their leading scorer restored, and a team that keeps finding the net. If Pulišić and Balogun click and Seattle roars, the co-hosts can absolutely send a European heavyweight home and book that SoFi date.

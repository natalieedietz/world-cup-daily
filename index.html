Done and Dusted · Today's Early Result

Norway open the day by making history and then immediately making everyone laugh.

Full Time
Round of 32 · Arlington (Dallas)
CIV Ivory Coast 1–2 Norway NOR
Haaland's 86th-minute winner sends Norway through. First knockout win in their history.

We called it a coin-flip, and for 85 minutes it absolutely was. Antonio Nusa nudged Norway ahead, then Amad Diallo equalized for Ivory Coast with a slick give-and-go off Nicolas Pépé and a cheeky little shimmy in the box, the sort of dance that says "I have this completely under control" right up until the moment you do not. Because then Erling Haaland happened. Off a Patrick Berg pass, the big man casually flicked the ball into an open net with his left foot, as if swatting a fly, his third goal in three games, and Norway had their first World Cup knockout win in the nation's entire history. Then came the celebration. The whole team sat down on the pitch and mimed rowing a Viking longboat in perfect synchronization. We are not joking. It immediately went viral. Ten out of ten, no notes, the Norwegians have invented joy. Ivory Coast, one of Africa's best, go home gutted, undone by the one man on the pitch who needs precisely zero chances to score.

First, What You Missed · Monday 29 June

A bloodbath. Two of the sport's aristocrats were marched out on penalties within hours of each other.

Giant Down Round of 32 · Foxborough · pens
GER Germany1–1Paraguay PAR

Germany, four-time world champions, are out in the Round of 32, and the manner could not have been more on-brand. Paraguay did exactly what we promised they would, parked roughly nine buses, dragged the game to penalties, and then won the shootout 4-3 while Germany's players rediscovered their national tradition of looking haunted from twelve yards. That is three straight World Cups of German misery. At this point it is less a slump and more a lifestyle.

Giant Down Round of 32 · Monterrey · pens
NED Netherlands1–1Morocco MAR

Not to be outdone, the Netherlands also packed it in, beaten on penalties by a Morocco side that simply does not know how to lose a knockout game in regulation. The Atlas Lions won the shootout 3-2, proving their run to the 2022 semifinals was no fluke and sending the three-time finalists home. The Dutch have now lost three World Cup finals and exited this one in the last 32. Somewhere, a tulip wilted.

Round of 32 · Houston
BRA Brazil2–1Japan JPN

The lone favorite who actually held up their end of the bargain, and even they had to sweat through their jersey. Japan, fearless as ever, took the lead and made Brazil look distinctly ordinary for a half before the Seleção remembered who they were, equalizing on 56 minutes and nicking a stoppage-time winner. Neymar never left the bench, which somehow felt like a flex. Brazil survive; Japan exit with their heads sky high.

Let us tally the bracket's body count so far. Already eliminated from the knockouts: South Africa, Japan, Germany, the Netherlands, and now Ivory Coast. The lesson of this Round of 32 is brutally simple: reputation buys you nothing once the whistle blows. Eat well, defend deep, take your penalties. The rest is vibes.

The Storylines

The Viking Row, Explained

After beating Ivory Coast, the entire Norway team sat down on the pitch in a line and mimed rowing a longboat, oars and all, in perfect rhythm. It is the kind of celebration that could only come from a nation that is fully at peace with itself. Haaland scoring in his third straight game is terrifying; Haaland scoring and then rowing an imaginary boat to the Round of 16 is the single most wholesome thing this tournament has produced. Brazil await on July 5. Bring the oars.

One pundit suggested the only way Sweden stop France is to lock the French team inside the dressing room before kickoff, and honestly, that feels like their best tactical option. Didier Deschamps' side, back with their manager after a personal absence, are the most complete team left in the bracket: they won all three group games, capped by a 4-1 dismantling of Norway in which Ousmane Dembélé, the reigning Ballon d'Or holder, helped himself to a hat-trick. Add Kylian Mbappé, Michael Olise and a bench deeper than most starting XIs, and you have a side that expects to be back at this very stadium for the final on July 19.

Sweden, bless them, arrive having conceded seven goals in the group stage, including a 5-1 mauling by the Netherlands, and qualified only as one of the best third-placed teams, which is the football equivalent of getting into the party because the bouncer felt sorry for you. They have genuine talent up front in Alexander Isak and Viktor Gyökeres, and on a perfect day they might nick one. But asking this defense to contain this French attack for 90 minutes is like asking a screen door to keep out a hurricane.

The only real intrigue is the final margin and whether Mbappé, sitting three goals shy of Messi's all-time World Cup record of 19, treats this as the open goal it appears to be. France should win, probably comfortably, possibly emphatically, and they will want to send a message to the rest of the bracket while they are at it. Watch for the record chase, watch for Dembélé tormenting full-backs, and watch Sweden discover that there are worse things than elimination, like elimination by five.

The nightcap arrives soaked in history and decibels. Mexico have been quietly superb, winning Group A with a perfect nine points and, remarkably, becoming the only team left in the entire tournament yet to concede a single goal. Now they return to the cathedral of the Estadio Azteca, where the noise is physical and the weight of expectation heavier still, chasing only the second World Cup knockout win in their history. The first, a 2-0 defeat of Bulgaria, came at this very stadium in 1986, almost exactly 40 years and two weeks ago. Mexican fans have been waiting four decades to exhale. No pressure.

Ecuador, though, are the precise opponent you do not want when you crave a calm evening. La Tri are built on a granite defensive structure marshaled by Chelsea's Moisés Caicedo, they upset Germany in the group stage just to prove they bite, and they have the discipline and athleticism to make a fortress feel like a trap. A tense, cagey, low-scoring affair suits them perfectly, and if they can survive the early Azteca tidal wave and keep it level, the famous Mexican knockout anxiety, a genuine national pastime, may begin to creep in.

Expect defense to write the story. Mexico's spotless record against Ecuador's miserly back line points to a taut, nervy night where one set piece, one flash of Santiago Giménez quality, or one defensive blink decides everything. The hosts are marginal favorites with 87,000 lungs behind them, but after yesterday's penalty carnage, would anyone be brave enough to rule out extra time and a shootout? The Azteca will not breathe easy until it is over. Possibly not even then.

Upset Watch

Very much alive. Ecuador's defense is good enough to smother this and drag a jittery Mexico into the lottery, where home expectation curdles into dread. If La Tri weather the opening storm and pinch one on the break, the Azteca's dream night could turn into a very familiar Mexican nightmare.

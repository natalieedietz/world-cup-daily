First, What You Missed · Wednesday 1 July

The USMNT advanced, Belgium performed a daylight robbery, and Harry Kane did Harry Kane things.

Full Time
Round of 32 · Santa Clara (Levi's)
USA USA 2–0 Bosnia & H. BIH
First USMNT knockout win since 2002. Belgium await in Seattle on Monday.

The USMNT reached the Round of 16 and could not resist adding a heart attack for the neighbors. Folarin Balogun opened the scoring and then, in a plot twist nobody ordered, promptly got himself sent off after a video review, leaving the co-hosts to defend for the final twenty-five-odd minutes with ten men and their most dangerous forward watching from the tunnel. Cue much nervous shouting across the Bay Area. But the defense held, Malik Tillman calmly buried a second in the 82nd minute for breathing room, and the USA sealed a 2-0 win, their first knockout victory since 2002. The reward is Belgium in Seattle on Monday. The nation exhaled. Pochettino aged four years.

Daylight Robbery Round of 32 · Seattle · a.e.t.
BEL Belgium3–2Senegal SEN

Absolute pandemonium in Seattle. Senegal led 2-0 and were cruising, having outplayed a limp Belgium for an hour, when the Red Devils remembered they were supposed to be good. Romelu Lukaku and Youri Tielemans struck in the 86th and 89th minutes to force extra time, and then Tielemans buried a deeply controversial stoppage-time penalty to complete a 3-2 heist. Senegal created the better chances all night and are going home. Football, as ever, is a cruel and beautiful accountant.

Round of 32 · Atlanta
ENG England2–1DR Congo COD

England, true to form, made a meal of it. Brian Cipenga gave DR Congo a shock early lead, Yoane Wissa rattled the woodwork, and the Cinderella dream was very much alive until Harry Kane decided enough was enough. The captain headed an equalizer in the 75th minute and then curled in a twisting winner on 86 to spare English blushes and complete a 2-1 comeback. The Leopards were magnificent and unlucky. England advance, sweating, as is tradition.

The Storylines

The World Cup, In Our Backyard

This is the one to circle. The European champions Spain, all silky passing and generational talent, play a genuine World Cup knockout tie at SoFi in Inglewood at noon. Lamine Yamal, the teenage phenomenon who is somehow already one of the best players on earth, will be strutting his stuff fifteen minutes down the freeway. Whether you go, watch at a bar, or just bask in the fact that it is happening in our backyard, this is peak "the World Cup lives here now" energy. Do not waste it.

Club Connections

Loads to recognize today. Spain field Lamine Yamal (Barcelona) and Pedri (Barcelona); Austria bring Marcel Sabitzer (Borussia Dortmund) and Konrad Laimer (Bayern). Portugal parade Cristiano Ronaldo (Al-Nassr) and Bruno Fernandes (Man Utd) against Croatia's ageless Luka Modrić. Switzerland lean on Granit Xhaka (Sunderland) and Manuel Akanji (Man City), with Algeria's Riyad Mahrez still pulling the strings.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

The whole day is in prime West Coast hours, and the noon SoFi game is unmissable simply because it is here. But save some energy for the 4pm Portugal v Croatia, a heavyweight clash and a farewell tour for two of the greatest to ever do it. And the 8pm Switzerland v Algeria is the sneaky-good nightcap, a proper coin-flip featuring the grudge-match survivors from that wild 3-3 with Austria. Fire up the group chat.

Adopt-a-Team Watch

Lend your underdog heart to Algeria, the Desert Foxes, who somehow survived that ludicrous 3-3 with Austria to reach the knockouts and would love nothing more than to spoil Switzerland's evening in Vancouver. With the veteran magician Riyad Mahrez still capable of moments of genius, they are the most watchable outsider left on today's card. A shock in Canada would keep the tournament's chaos quota topped up nicely. Lean in.

The good news for the neighborhood: the best passing team in the world is coming to Inglewood. Spain, the reigning European champions, have quietly rounded into ominous form, keeping clean sheets against Uruguay and Saudi Arabia and generally making the ball do the running while everyone else chases shadows. Their teenage talisman Lamine Yamal has been getting healthier and sharper, Pedri conducts the midfield like a maestro who finds tackling beneath him, and on their day Spain can pass an opponent into a quiet, dignified oblivion. They are among the genuine favorites to win the whole thing.

Austria arrive as heavy underdogs, but let us not pretend they are here by accident. They reached the knockouts via that gloriously unhinged 3-3 draw with Algeria, they have real quality in Marcel Sabitzer and Konrad Laimer, and they are physical, organized and entirely capable of making Spain's afternoon a frustrating, niggly slog. Their game plan is obvious: sit deep, stay compact, foul the tippy-tappy out of the Spanish rhythm, and pray they can nick something on a set piece or a counter. It has worked against better teams than them.

The overwhelming likelihood is a Spanish win, probably a controlled and comfortable one, with the only real danger being the same complacency trap that has already swallowed Germany and the Netherlands this week. If Spain score early, this could become an exhibition. If Austria frustrate them into a goalless first hour, the SoFi crowd will get nervous and Austria will start to believe. Expect Spain to advance and to look very good doing it, and enjoy the fact that world-class football is unfolding, quite literally, on your doorstep.

Upset Watch

Faint, but this is knockout week, when faint has been getting the job done. Austria will defend deep and hope to drag Spain into the kind of stodgy, low-scoring grind where one set piece changes everything. It would take a near-perfect Austrian night and an off-color Spain, but stranger things have already happened in the last seven days.

Portugal v Croatia

Savor this one, because we will not see its like again. Cristiano Ronaldo, 41 and still refusing to acknowledge the calendar, leads a talented Portugal side that has flattered to deceive so far, stumbling to a limp goalless draw with Colombia to end the group after Ronaldo finally found his shooting boots against Uzbekistan. Portugal have the deeper, more dynamic squad, with Bruno Fernandes pulling strings and genuine pace all over the pitch. On talent alone they should have too much. The question, as ever with Ronaldo-era Portugal, is whether the sum matches the parts.

Croatia, meanwhile, are running on the timeless genius of Luka Modrić, who at 40 continues to glide around midfields as if the aging process is something that happens to other people. This is a Croatia side past its absolute peak, but nobody in world football is better at grinding out tight knockout games, absorbing pressure, and dragging opponents into the extra time and penalties they have made their spiritual home. Write them off at your peril; they have made a decade-long habit of outlasting flashier teams when it matters most.

Portugal are rightly favored on squad depth and firepower, but this is far closer to a real contest than the outright odds suggest, and it comes wrapped in genuine emotional weight: two all-time greats, almost certainly meeting on this stage for the last time. Expect a tense, cagey, high-quality affair that could easily be decided by a single moment of Ronaldo ruthlessness or Modrić magic, and do not rule out it going the distance, because Croatia would like nothing more. A fitting farewell duel for two legends.

Upset Watch

Very real. Croatia are underdogs by reputation but are arguably the best team left at winning ugly, and Portugal have looked disjointed and Ronaldo-dependent. If Modrić controls the tempo and drags this to extra time, the smart money knows exactly which of these two nations has ice in its veins from twelve yards.

The nightcap is quietly one of the most even ties of the round. Switzerland are the epitome of tournament competence: organized, disciplined, defensively sound, and captained by the combative Granit Xhaka, with Manchester City's Manuel Akanji anchoring a back line that does not give much away. They rarely blow anyone off the park, but they equally rarely beat themselves, and that kind of unfussy reliability is exactly what wins tight knockout games. They are marginal favorites for good reason.

Algeria, on the other hand, are pure box-office chaos. The Desert Foxes reached this stage via that berserk 3-3 draw with Austria, they play with flair and abandon, and in the veteran Riyad Mahrez they have a genuine magician still capable of conjuring a goal from nothing. They can be defensively porous and occasionally maddening, but they are never, ever boring, and on their day they have the attacking talent to trouble a Swiss side that prefers a slower, more controlled game. Style versus substance, essentially.

This one is close to a genuine coin-flip, the classic contrast between a team that wants to keep it tidy and a team that wants to turn it into a carnival. Switzerland will try to smother the game and squeeze the life out of Algeria's flair; Algeria will try to crank up the tempo and drag the Swiss into a shoot-out. Expect a tight, tense affair with a real chance of extra time, and if you have made it through a full day of football to the 8pm kickoff, this is a worthy reward. A proper toss-up to end the night.

Upset Watch

Barely an upset given how level this is. Algeria are underdogs only by the thinnest margin, and Mahrez is exactly the kind of player who wins knockout games on his own. If they can drag the disciplined Swiss into an open, chaotic contest, the Desert Foxes are more than capable of advancing.

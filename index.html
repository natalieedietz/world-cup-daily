First, What You Missed · Tuesday 30 June

France flexed, Norway rowed, and a thunderstorm tried to referee a knockout game.

Round of 32 · East Rutherford
FRA France 3–0 Sweden SWE
Mbappé brace ties Messi for the Golden Boot. France look ominous.

We said the only way Sweden stop France was to lock the French team in the dressing room, and Sweden, tragically, forgot to bring a key. It took 45 minutes for Kylian Mbappé to finally crack a stubborn Swedish wall, but once he did, the floodgates opened: two Mbappé goals, a Bradley Barcola strike teed up by a Michael Olise nutmeg so casually cruel it deserves its own documentary, and a comfortable 3-0. Mbappé's brace pulls him level with Lionel Messi on six goals for the Golden Boot, setting up a race for the ages. Olise also nearly scored an overhead kick that hit the post, which would have been the goal of the tournament and is the only thing Sweden managed to prevent all evening.

History · and Oars Round of 32 · Arlington
CIV Ivory Coast1–2Norway NOR

Erling Haaland scored in his third straight game, an 86th-minute winner flicked in "like swatting a fly," to send Norway to their first World Cup knockout win ever. Then the entire squad sat down and mimed rowing a Viking longboat in perfect unison, which remains the single greatest thing to happen at this tournament. Their reward for all this joy? Brazil, on Sunday. The oars may need reinforcing.

Round of 32 · Mexico City
Mexico v EcuadorStorm-delayed

The nightcap at the Azteca ran into a literal act of God. A thunderstorm rolled over Mexico City and FIFA pushed kickoff back by an hour, banned hydration breaks, and generally reminded everyone that no amount of money can bully the weather. The match kicked off late into the night, so if you were asleep on the West Coast, you are forgiven. Check the score with your morning coffee: the winner faces the survivor of today's England v DR Congo tie.

Buckle up, because the two best number-in-a-generation forwards are now dead level at the top of the scoring charts, both on six. Haaland lurks just behind on the leaderboard, presumably plotting his next celebration. The Golden Boot is now a genuine three-way heavyweight bout.

The Storylines

Miracle on Grass?

Mauricio Pochettino has reportedly been showing his USMNT squad the Miracle on Ice, the 1980 US hockey team's shock win over the Soviet Union. It is a rousing story, with one small wrinkle: in that film the Americans were massive underdogs, and tonight the USA are clear favorites against Bosnia. So it is less "plucky underdogs shock the world" and more "please, for the love of everything, do not overthink a game you are supposed to win." The USMNT have a habit of making the simple things spicy. Tonight, simple would be nice.

Club Connections

Plenty of Premier League flavor today. England field a squad stuffed with it: Harry Kane (Bayern), Jude Bellingham (Real Madrid), Bukayo Saka (Arsenal), Declan Rice (Arsenal). Belgium bring Jeremy Doku (Man City) and Youri Tielemans (Aston Villa); Senegal counter with Ismaila Sarr (Crystal Palace). The USA lean on Christian Pulisic (AC Milan) and Weston McKennie (Juventus), with DR Congo's Yoane Wissa (Newcastle) leading the Cinderella charge.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

The whole day is in prime West Coast hours, which is frankly rude of the schedule in the best way. The main event is unmissable: USA v Bosnia at 5pm, a co-host chasing the last 16 in front of a heaving Bay Area crowd. But do not sleep on Belgium v Senegal at 1pm, two teams who both put five goals past their final group opponents and defend like the door is optional. That one could be a track meet. Fire up the group chat.

Adopt-a-Team Watch

Point your underdog heart at DR Congo, the Leopards, through to the knockouts for the very first time in their history and utterly unbothered by England's star-studded roster. They are rank outsiders, they will defend for their lives, and they have already shown they can score. If they nick a result against a strangely creativity-starved England, it would be one of the great World Cup shocks. Also acceptable: quietly enjoying Bosnia making the USMNT sweat. We will not tell.

England arrive in Atlanta doing their most authentic England impression: clearly the better team on paper, and somehow making everyone nervous about it anyway. Thomas Tuchel's side topped a tricky Group L, beating Croatia 4-2 and Panama 2-0, but sandwiched a limp goalless draw with Ghana in between, and the enduring theme has been a strange lack of creative spark for a team overflowing with attacking talent. Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham, Bukayo Saka and Marcus Rashford should be too much for anyone at this level. The question, as ever with England, is whether they can be bothered to make it look easy.

DR Congo, meanwhile, are living their best life. The Leopards have reached the knockout stage of a World Cup for the first time in their entire history, and they arrive with absolutely nothing to lose and a defense that intends to make England's afternoon deeply irritating. Yoane Wissa of Newcastle gives them a genuine outlet, and if they can frustrate England into another one of those grim, sparkless afternoons, the belief will grow with every passing minute. Cinderella stories need a stubborn back line and a bit of luck, and DR Congo have packed both.

The likeliest outcome remains a controlled England win, their big-game players eventually prising open a stubborn lock, with the smart money on a low-scoring, faintly tense afternoon rather than a rout. But England have made "harder than it needed to be" into an art form, and if DR Congo score first, the Atlanta crowd will smell an upset and England's famous tournament anxiety will come flooding back. Expect the favorites to advance. Expect them to make you sweat through at least one cup of coffee first.

Upset Watch

Modest but real, because it is England. DR Congo will defend deep, spring the odd counter through Wissa, and hope to drag a creativity-shy England side into a nervy, goalless grind. If it is still level past the hour, the pressure lands entirely on the favorites, and England know exactly how to make that uncomfortable.

If you like goals and dislike defending, clear your afternoon. Belgium and Senegal arrive as two of the most watchable, most gung-ho teams left in the bracket, and the numbers are delicious: Belgium hammered New Zealand 5-1 to win their group, Senegal understood the assignment and demolished Iraq 5-0 to sneak through as a best third-placed side. Both teams love to pour forward in numbers, and both, crucially, get a little seasick when they lose the ball. On paper this is less a chess match and more a pillow fight with knives.

Belgium, marginal favorites, still carry serious quality even as their so-called golden generation ages: Jeremy Doku is a genuine game-breaker on the wing, and everything flows through Aston Villa's Youri Tielemans in midfield. Stop Tielemans and you slow the whole Belgian machine, which is easier said than done. Senegal, for their part, have the athleticism and firepower to trade blows with anyone, and Crystal Palace's Ismaila Sarr has been feeling this tournament, exactly the kind of in-form winger who can win a shoot-out on his own.

The bookmakers call this the closest tie of the day for a reason: it is essentially a coin-flip with a high ceiling. Expect both teams to attack, expect defensive lapses at both ends, and expect the very real possibility that whoever has the ball last simply wins. If it stays tight it could creep toward extra time, but the more likely script is an open, breathless, goal-happy afternoon. Of today's undercard, this is the one to actually sit down and watch.

Upset Watch

Barely an upset, given how level this is. Senegal are marginal underdogs by reputation only; their pace and finishing can overwhelm a Belgian defense that has looked shaky. If this turns into the track meet everyone expects, Senegal are more than capable of winning a goals-galore shoot-out.

Here it is, the whole point of the day for the home crowd. The USMNT reach the knockout rounds and get a winnable but genuinely tricky tie against Bosnia and Herzegovina, with a booming Bay Area crowd firmly on their side. Mauricio Pochettino's side stumbled to the finish line, losing a last-gasp 3-2 thriller to Türkiye, but they got out of the group and now face a Bosnia team ranked below them and, on paper, there for the taking. Christian Pulisic and Weston McKennie carry the creative load, and on their day the USA have more than enough to win this comfortably.

The catch, and there is always a catch with this team, is that Bosnia are precisely the kind of gritty, unbothered opponent who can spoil a party. They beat Italy in qualifying just to reach this tournament, which is the sort of scalp that removes any fear of a big name or a hostile stadium. The USA, awkwardly, have not beaten a UEFA opponent since December 2021, and the last team they beat was, delightfully, Bosnia. History rhymes, but it does not guarantee. If the Americans are sloppy or nervous, Bosnia have the nous to punish them.

Pochettino, in a move that is either inspired or mildly concerning, has reportedly been leaning on the Miracle on Ice for motivation, which works better as a story when your team is not the favorite. The likeliest outcome is a controlled American win powered by the crowd and by Pulisic's quality, but knockout football and this particular team's flair for unnecessary drama mean nobody in a USA shirt should relax. Expect nerves, expect noise, and expect the co-hosts to advance, probably the hard way, because that is simply how they do things.

Upset Watch

Live, and the USMNT know it. Bosnia beat Italy to get here and will not be fazed by the crowd or the occasion. If the USA are jittery early or fail to convert their chances, this becomes exactly the kind of tense, low-scoring night where one Bosnian moment, or a shootout, could send the co-hosts home to a nation of groans.

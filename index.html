First, What You Missed · Monday 6 July

Spain struck at the death, a legend bowed out, and the USA ran into a Belgian buzzsaw.

Late, Late Show
Round of 16 · Arlington
ESP Spain 1–0 Portugal POR
Merino strikes in stoppage time. Ronaldo's World Cup career comes to a close.

Spain did everything but score for 90 minutes, dominating Portugal so thoroughly that the only surprise was the scoreboard staying blank, and then, mere seconds into stoppage time, Mikel Merino slid onto a gorgeous pass and beat Diogo Costa to break Portuguese hearts. Spain win 1-0 and roll into a SoFi quarterfinal. The bigger story was on the other side: Cristiano Ronaldo, who had said this would be his final World Cup, walked off the stage for the last time at 41, his tournament career ending in defeat. Whatever your allegiances, that is the closing of one of the sport's great chapters. Spain march on. A titan takes his final bow.

Tough Night Round of 16 · Seattle
BEL Belgium4–1USA USA

The dream of a home run ended abruptly. The USA, cheered on by a raucous Seattle crowd and boosted by Folarin Balogun's late reprieve from suspension, simply could not live with Belgium, who pounced on a series of defensive errors to win 4-1 and reach the quarterfinals. Romelu Lukaku added the fourth to seal it, and the air went out of Lumen Field. It is a deflating end to a spirited USMNT tournament run, but a genuinely encouraging one to build on. Belgium go to SoFi. The co-hosts go home with their heads high.

The Storylines

A Duel of the Number Tens

This is a poster made real: Lionel Messi against Mohamed Salah, two of the finest attackers of their generation, sharing a pitch with a quarterfinal on the line. Messi, at 39, is leading the Golden Boot race with seven goals and playing like a man who has decided he is not done being the best player alive. Salah is Egypt's everything, the talisman carrying a nation's 92-year wait on his back. One is the overwhelming favorite, one is the romantic hope, and for 90-plus minutes we get to watch them try to outshine each other. Cherish it.

Club Connections

Star wattage everywhere. Argentina bring Lionel Messi (Inter Miami) and Lautaro Martínez (Inter Milan); Egypt counter with Mohamed Salah (Liverpool) and Omar Marmoush (Man City). In Vancouver, Colombia lean on Luis Díaz (Bayern Munich) and the veteran James Rodríguez, while Switzerland answer with Granit Xhaka (Sunderland) and Manchester City's Manuel Akanji anchoring a typically stingy back line.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

Both games land in comfortable West Coast hours, so you can enjoy the lot. The 9am Argentina v Egypt is unmissable for the Messi-Salah spectacle alone, even if the result feels heavily pre-written. The 1pm Colombia v Switzerland is the sneaky-good one, two well-drilled sides in a genuinely even contest for a quarterfinal spot, exactly the kind of tie that quietly turns into a classic while everyone is looking elsewhere. Fire up the group chat.

History Watch · Egypt's 92-Year Wait

Spare a thought for the romance of Egypt. The Pharaohs reached the Round of 16 for the first time since 1934, an astonishing 92-year gap, and they got here on genuine merit, surviving a penalty shootout against Australia in which Salah cheekily chipped a Panenka down the middle. Beat Argentina and they would become just the fifth African nation ever to reach a World Cup quarterfinal. The odds say no. History, occasionally, does not care what the odds say. One can dream.

Let us not overcomplicate the football: Argentina should win, and win fairly comfortably. The defending champions have won all four of their matches, Lionel Messi is playing like a man who has personally taken offense to the passage of time, sitting atop the Golden Boot race with seven goals, and Lautaro Martínez gives them a lethal focal point through the middle. The only genuine scare they have had all tournament was that ludicrous extra-time survival act against Cabo Verde, and even that they eventually navigated. This is a deep, balanced, ruthless side that knows precisely how to win knockout games.

Egypt, bless them, are here on romance and Mohamed Salah. Reaching the last 16 for the first time since 1934 is a monumental achievement, but the manner of it, grinding, cagey, reliant on penalties against Australia, tells you they are light on goals and have leaked at the back all tournament, conceding in six straight matches. Their game plan is obvious and entirely reasonable: defend deep, keep it tight, frustrate Argentina for as long as humanly possible, and hope Salah can conjure one moment of magic on the counter. It is a long shot, and they know it, but a nation's dream does not require good odds to be worth chasing.

The overwhelming likelihood is a measured Argentine win, probably by a couple of goals, with Messi decorating it at some point because that is simply what he does now. The joy here is not in the suspense of the result but in the spectacle: two magnificent number tens, one chasing back-to-back World Cups and the other carrying a continent's hopes, sharing a stage one more time. Watch for the Messi-Salah subplot, savor the individual brilliance, and if Egypt somehow keep it level into the final stretch, the tension will become genuinely delicious. Enjoy the icons.

Upset Watch

Slim, and Egypt know it. Their only path is to defend heroically, smother Messi, and steal a moment through Salah on the break. They have the talisman to do it, but a defense that has conceded in six straight games is a shaky foundation for shocking the world champions. It would take a near-perfect night and a rare off-day from Messi.

While everyone is glued to Messi in the morning, this is the tie that could quietly steal the day. Colombia have been one of the tournament's understated successes, topping a group that contained Portugal (whom they held to a goalless draw) and looking organized, balanced and dangerous throughout. Luis Díaz provides the electric cutting edge, the evergreen James Rodríguez supplies the guile, and they have a defense that has barely been breached since their opening match. They are marginal favorites, and they carry the quiet confidence of a side that fancies a deep run.

Switzerland, though, are the ultimate tournament nuisance, and they arrive on a genuine high, having won three consecutive World Cup matches for the first time in their history. They are everything you expect from the Swiss: disciplined, defensively watertight, superbly organized, and captained by the combative Granit Xhaka, with Manchester City's Manuel Akanji marshaling the back line. They will not blow Colombia away, but they rarely beat themselves either, and that maddening reliability is exactly what turns tight knockout ties into penalty shootouts. Underestimate them at your peril.

This is comfortably the most even tie of the round, a classic clash of styles: Colombian flair and attacking verve against Swiss structure and stubbornness. Expect a tight, tense, tactical affair where the first goal carries enormous weight, and do not be at all surprised if it goes the distance, because Switzerland would happily drag anyone into extra time and penalties. Whoever survives earns a quarterfinal and a serious claim to be a genuine dark horse. This is the one the neutrals will thank themselves for watching. Do not sleep on it.

Upset Watch

Barely an upset, that is how level this is. Switzerland are slim underdogs only because Colombia have marginally more attacking flair. If the Swiss do what they do best, smother the game, frustrate Díaz, and drag it toward penalties, they are more than capable of advancing. This one genuinely could go either way, and often those are the best watches.

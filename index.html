The Storylines

The Grudge Match to End Them All

Argentina versus England is not a football match, it is a forty-year-old scar with a kickoff time. This is the fixture of Maradona's "Hand of God" in 1986, of David Beckham's red card in 1998, of decades of mutual and enthusiastic loathing. England have not beaten Argentina at a men's World Cup since 2002. Now they meet with a place in the final at stake, Messi against Bellingham, history against hunger. If you watch one game this week, make it this one. The tension will be visible from orbit.

Where to even begin. Argentina against England is one of the sport's great blood feuds, a fixture drenched in four decades of history: Maradona's "Hand of God" and his miraculous solo goal in 1986, David Beckham's red card and subsequent national villain era in 1998, then Beckham's redemption penalty in 2002. England have not beaten Argentina at a men's World Cup since that night, and now the two nations meet in a semifinal, with a place in the final and roughly a century of grudges on the line. You could bottle the tension in Atlanta and sell it as an energy drink.

The football is fascinating too. Argentina, the defending champions, have been the tournament's great survivors, needing extra time in three of their four knockout ties and generally making life as difficult for themselves as humanly possible, but they keep winning, because they have Lionel Messi. His record scoring streak ended against Switzerland, yet he still produced arguably the individual performance of the round, pulling every string and setting up the opener. England, meanwhile, are quietly the form horse nobody quite trusts: Tuchel's side have matched their best-ever World Cup with five wins, riding Jude Bellingham's heroics and Harry Kane's goals, even as their own manager keeps grumbling that they are "sloppy." Bellingham has back-to-back knockout braces, a feat last managed by, of all people, Maradona in 1986. The universe is not subtle.

The bookmakers make England slight favorites, a genuinely rare thing against Argentina, largely on squad depth, midfield power, and the champions' alarming habit of living dangerously. But Argentina have Messi, tournament know-how, and the priceless ability to win ugly when it matters, and they will relish being underestimated one more time. Expect a tense, cagey, emotionally enormous occasion where a single moment settles everything, and do not rule out extra time or penalties, because these two do not do simple. The prize is a final against a Spain side that just made France look like a pub team. History will be made either way.

Upset Watch

The twist is that Argentina, the defending champions, are the slight underdogs here, which tells you how much England are fancied. But underestimating Messi in a knockout game has ended badly for two decades of opponents. If he conjures one moment, or the champions drag it to the penalties they have mastered, England's long history of semifinal heartbreak could come calling at the cruelest possible time.

Canada's first-ever World Cup knockout win.

The knockouts opened on our doorstep and delivered a slow-burn thriller. South Africa, true to their entire tournament, defended like men guarding the last bread in a famine, and for long stretches it looked like they might drag the favorites to extra time and beyond. Then, late, Canada finally found the breakthrough, and with it became the first Canadian team in history to win a World Cup knockout match and reach the last 16. Jesse Marsch, the coach his own country passed over, is now one win from a quarter-final. Bafana go home with their heads high and a tournament to be proud of. SoFi roared.

The Storylines

The Dark Horse Problem

Brazil are the five-time champions and overwhelming favorites, but they have drawn the one team nobody wanted: Japan, who at the last World Cup beat both Germany and Spain in the same tournament, just to prove the first one was not a fluke. The Samurai Blue play fearless, fluid, ferociously organized football, and they will not be remotely starstruck. If Brazil are even slightly off, this is the banana skin of the entire round.

Club Connections

A loaded cast today. Brazil parade Vinícius Júnior and Rodrygo (both Real Madrid) plus Raphinha (Barcelona); Japan counter with Kaoru Mitoma (Brighton) and Takefusa Kubo (Real Sociedad). Germany run through Florian Wirtz (Liverpool), Jamal Musiala (Bayern) and Kai Havertz (Arsenal). The Netherlands lean on Virgil van Dijk and Cody Gakpo (both Liverpool), with Morocco's Achraf Hakimi (PSG) and Brahim Díaz (Real Madrid) headlining the pick of the round.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

Good news for the West Coast: the action runs 10am to evening, all in prime LA hours. If you watch one game start to finish, make it the 6pm Netherlands v Morocco, a near coin-flip between two genuine quarter-final contenders and a spiritual rematch of Morocco's stunning 2022 run. But settle in early for Brazil v Japan at 10am, because if there is going to be a giant-killing today, that is where it starts. Fire up the group chat.

Adopt-a-Team Watch

With the islanders' run paused for the day, point your underdog love at Morocco, the Atlas Lions, who in 2022 became the first African and Arab nation ever to reach a World Cup semifinal, dumping out Spain and Portugal along the way. They are not really underdogs anymore, they are a genuine force, and a deep run from them electrifies fans from Casablanca to, frankly, all over LA. Tonight they get the Dutch. Lean in.

Brazil v Japan

The five-time champions against the dark horse who has made a career of humbling giants. Brazil are favorites, but Japan are precisely the wrong opponent to take lightly.

Here is the tie the Brazilian camp will privately have dreaded. On paper it is a mismatch: Brazil are the most successful nation in World Cup history, with a front line of Vinícius Júnior, Rodrygo and Raphinha that can take any defense apart, and under Carlo Ancelotti they have rounded into ominous form after a sluggish start, topping their group and looking every inch contenders. They have the talent, the pedigree and the swagger. They are, rightly, heavy favorites to win this and march deep into the bracket.

And yet. Japan are not here to admire the scenery. At the 2022 World Cup, the Samurai Blue did something almost nobody manages, beating both Germany and Spain in the same group, two former world champions dispatched by a side that presses in coordinated waves and counters like a thunderclap. They drew with the Netherlands and dismantled Tunisia in this tournament, Kaoru Mitoma and Takefusa Kubo provide genuine match-winning quality, and they will treat Brazil not as untouchable gods but as simply the next big name to be toppled. Neymar, back in the Brazil fold, has called Japan a favorite opponent, which is the kind of thing that ages either beautifully or catastrophically.

The likeliest outcome remains a Brazilian win, perhaps even a comfortable one if Vinícius gets isolated against the full-back and the samba machine clicks. But knockout football strips away the cushion of a second chance, and Japan are tailor-made to exploit any complacency. If they score first and make Brazil anxious, Houston could witness the upset of the tournament. This is the must-watch opener for a reason: the favorite is brilliant, and the underdog has a very specific, very recent history of eating brilliant favorites alive.

Upset Watch

Among the liveliest of the round. Japan have beaten Germany and Spain on this stage in living memory, and a single early goal could turn Brazilian flair into Brazilian panic. If the Samurai Blue press high and Brazil's defense blinks, do not be remotely surprised to see a giant fall in Houston.

Germany v Paraguay

Germany arrive embarrassed by Ecuador and desperate to look serious again. Paraguay arrive ready to make the game as joyless and ugly as humanly possible. A clash of philosophies.

Germany come into the knockouts carrying a faint whiff of scandal, having lost 2-1 to Ecuador in their final group game, a result that mattered nothing to their qualification and everything to their dignity. They had already won the group on the back of a thunderous 7-1 demolition of Curaçao, and the talent is undeniable, Florian Wirtz and Jamal Musiala forming one of the most gifted creative axes in world football, with Kai Havertz leading the line. But Germany have made an art form of group-stage swagger followed by knockout heartbreak, and Julian Nagelsmann knows another flat performance here would reopen every old wound.

Paraguay, by delicious contrast, are here to ruin the party. They ground their way out of the group, most memorably by scoring against Türkiye inside 64 seconds and then defending grimly for the rest of the match, and then played out a cagey, goalless stalemate with Australia to clinch their spot. This is a team built on discipline, organization and the firm belief that the most beautiful thing in football is a clean sheet. They will sit deep, frustrate, foul when they must, and hope to drag Germany into the kind of low-scoring knife-fight where one set piece or one counter decides everything.

Germany are clear favorites and, on talent, should have far too much, especially since Nagelsmann has shown he prefers to attack his way out of trouble rather than grind. But knockout football and a stubborn South American low block are exactly the combination that has undone fancied European sides before, and if Germany are still goalless past the hour, the ghosts of 2018 and 2022 will start murmuring. Expect German pressure, expect Paraguayan resistance, and expect the tension to build with every minute the favorites fail to break through.

Upset Watch

Real, in the specific way knockout football allows. Paraguay will not try to win a shootout; they will try to strangle the game and steal it late or on penalties. Germany's recent tournament history is not exactly a vault of calm. If the breakthrough does not come early, the nerves will.

Netherlands v Morocco

Save your full attention for this one, because it is the jewel of the round. The Netherlands arrived as dark-horse contenders and have largely justified it, topping a tricky group that included Japan and crushing Sweden 5-1 along the way. Ronald Koeman has a side loaded with quality and balance, Virgil van Dijk marshalling the back, Cody Gakpo and Memphis Depay carrying the threat, and the kind of tournament know-how that wins tight knockout games. On their day, they can beat anyone left in the bracket, and they will fancy this.

But Morocco are no ordinary last-32 opponent, they are a side that fundamentally rewired what is possible for African football. In 2022 the Atlas Lions became the first African and Arab nation ever to reach a World Cup semifinal, and they did not sneak there: they knocked out Spain on penalties and beat Portugal outright, defending with a discipline and ferocity that left the world breathless. This is a deeper, more experienced version of that team, with Achraf Hakimi a genuine superstar at full-back and Brahim Díaz pulling strings. They will not fear the Dutch in the slightest, and they have the defensive steel and counterattacking venom to win any knockout game they play.

This is as close to a 50-50 as the round offers, two excellent sides who could both plausibly reach the semifinals, meeting absurdly early because of how the bracket fell. Expect a tactical chess match with moments of genuine quality, expect it to be tight, and expect the very real possibility of extra time and penalties, where Morocco's nerve has historically been ice-cold. If you watch one full ninety, or 120, minutes today, make it this. The winner announces themselves as a serious threat to go all the way.

Upset Watch

Barely an upset at all, given how level this is. Morocco are marginal underdogs only by reputation, not by quality; they have the better recent World Cup pedigree and a penalty-shootout temperament few can match. If this goes the distance, do not back against the Atlas Lions.

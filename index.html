First, What You Missed · Wednesday 24 June

Three groups finished, one giant fell, one fairytale ended, and one tiny nation lost a game and won everyone's heart.

Bracket Buster Group A · Monterrey
🇿🇦 South Africa1–0🇰🇷 South Korea

While Los Angeles slept, Bafana Bafana detonated the tournament. Thapelo Maseko's goal sank a South Korea side built around Son Heung-min, and South Africa, who hosted a World Cup in 2010 and still went home early, have reached the knockout rounds for the first time in their entire history. Son, who spends his weekends a few miles from your sofa playing for LAFC, trudged off eliminated. The reward for South Africa's heroics? A date with Canada on Sunday. The reward for the rest of us? Chaos, beautiful chaos.

Group C · Miami Gardens
🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scotland0–3🇧🇷 Brazil

And so the streak lives on. Asked to beat Brazil to be sure of survival, Scotland did the most Scottish thing imaginable and lost 3-0 to a Vinícius Júnior masterclass, then immediately had to start refreshing other groups' scores to see whether they sneak through as a best third-placed team. Eleven World Cups, still no clean escape from a group under their own steam. McTominay rattled the post in the 95th minute, because of course he did. The agony is the brand.

Heroes in Defeat Group C · Atlanta
🇲🇦 Morocco4–2🇭🇹 Haiti

Haiti, the lowest-ranked team in the entire tournament, were already eliminated and trailing comfortably, and then produced 45 of the most joyous minutes in their footballing history, scoring twice and very nearly conjuring a comeback that would have shaken the planet. Morocco, the 2022 semifinalists, eventually steadied themselves and advanced second. But the cameras stayed on the Haitian players at full time, applauding a crowd that adored them. They lost the match and won the day. Football, occasionally, gets it right.

Group A · Mexico City
🇲🇽 Mexico3–0🇨🇿 Czechia

Mexico signed off the group stage with a tidy 3-0 over Czechia at a delirious Azteca, where they have somehow never lost a World Cup match across three different tournaments, capped by a stoppage-time rocket from Fidalgo. In Group B, meanwhile, Switzerland beat Canada 2-1 to nick top spot, scoring twice early in the second half before Promise David's thunderbolt gave the co-hosts a nervy, ultimately academic, consolation. Both Swiss and Canadians are through.

Watch with one eyebrow permanently raised.

Group E · 1:00 PM PT · Lincoln Financial Field, Philadelphia

Island Dream ⚽ Amad · Man Utd

Curaçao v Ivory Coast

The smallest nation ever to play a World Cup, armed with one famous point and a goalkeeper who has been possessed, against an Ivory Coast side that simply must win to survive.

Curaçao 5% Draw 11% Ivory Coast 84%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Let us be clear-eyed and also romantic, because both are allowed. Ivory Coast are vastly the stronger side, they must win to keep their own knockout hopes alive after that gut-wrenching late loss to Germany, and they carry genuine Premier League menace in Manchester United's Amad Diallo and Brighton's Simon Adingra. By every rational measure, the Elephants win this, probably comfortably, and march on to the round of 32 as they were always expected to.

And yet here are Curaçao, population roughly 156,000, the smallest nation ever to grace a World Cup, sitting on a point nobody believed they would take, courtesy of goalkeeper Eloy Room producing a fifteen-save exorcism against Ecuador that the record books had not witnessed since 1966. They have already rewritten what is possible for a microstate at this tournament. To advance they likely need to win, which the odds rate at roughly the same probability as being struck by a particularly motivated meteor, but they have stopped caring what the odds think.

Realistically, the fairytale meets its limit today against a hungrier, more talented team that cannot afford to slip. But Curaçao have already turned a footnote into a headline, and if Room catches fire one more time and the islanders nick something, it would stand among the greatest underdog stories the World Cup has ever told. Adopt them for ninety more minutes. They have earned every neutral heart in the building.

Upset Watch

The result would be a colossal shock, but this is the team that already shocked everyone once. Ivory Coast's nerves matter here: a side that must win can tighten up, and if Room reprises his heroics, the pressure on the Elephants could become a story all its own.

Pick of the Day ⚽ Gyökeres · Arsenal ⚽ Mitoma · Brighton

Japan v Sweden

The tournament's favorite dark horse against two of the Premier League's deadliest forwards, with the top of Group F and serious bragging rights on the line.

Japan 51% Draw 27% Sweden 22%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

This is the connoisseur's match of the day. Japan have been quietly magnificent, drawing the Netherlands and then dismantling Tunisia 4-0, and they play the kind of fast, fluid, technically immaculate football that has made them the dark horse every shrewd observer keeps whispering about. Kaoru Mitoma and Takefusa Kubo conduct the orchestra, and a win here likely sends them through as Group F winners with a very dangerous reputation attached.

Sweden, by contrast, are a fascinating puzzle: blessed with arguably the most fearsome strike pairing at the entire tournament in Viktor Gyökeres and Alexander Isak, yet inconsistent enough to have been thrashed 5-1 by the Netherlands after hammering Tunisia themselves. They sit on three points and need a result to be sure of progressing, which means Graham Potter's side must finally make those two elite forwards count against a Japanese defense that is organized but not impregnable.

The contrast of styles is delicious: Japanese fluency and pressing against Swedish power and clinical finishing. Japan are narrow favorites and arguably the better team, but if Gyökeres and Isak get even a couple of clean looks, Sweden can win any game they play. Expect goals, expect swings, and expect this to be the most entertaining ninety minutes before the SoFi nightcap. If you sit down properly for one game today, make it this one.

Upset Watch

Barely an upset given how level this is. Sweden's front two can win it outright, but a Japan side this slick could equally run riot. The genuine intrigue is whether Swedish quality up top can outweigh Japanese control everywhere else.

Group F · 4:00 PM PT · Arrowhead Stadium, Kansas City

Formality ⚽ Van Dijk · Liverpool

Tunisia v Netherlands

An eliminated, badly bruised Tunisia against a Netherlands side that needs only to behave itself to top the group. The stakes are lopsided; the Dutch are not complaining.

Tunisia 4% Draw 9% Netherlands 87%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Spare a thought, or perhaps avert your eyes, for Tunisia. They arrived at this World Cup having conceded zero goals across their entire African qualifying campaign, a fortress of a defense, and then promptly shipped five to Sweden and four to Japan, conceding nine in two games and exiting with their tournament in rubble. Now, already eliminated, they face the Netherlands, which is the football equivalent of stubbing your toe and then being asked to run a marathon.

The Netherlands, meanwhile, are cruising. After a thrilling opening draw with Japan they obliterated Sweden 5-1, and they need only a competent afternoon to top Group F and set up a favourable knockout path. Ronald Koeman can rest legs if he chooses, with Virgil van Dijk marshalling things and Cody Gakpo and Memphis Depay providing the cutting edge. They will want goals to bolster their goal difference, but the result itself is close to a foregone conclusion.

Realistically this is target practice, and the only real questions are the margin and whether Tunisia can salvage a sliver of pride with a goal to take home. For the Dutch, it is about staying switched on, avoiding injuries, and arriving at the knockouts in rhythm. For Tunisia, it is about dignity, and the small, defiant hope of denying a giant a clean sheet on the way out the door.

Upset Watch

Vanishingly small. Tunisia have leaked nine goals in two games and are playing for nothing but pride. The only intrigue is whether they grab a consolation, and whether the Netherlands keep their concentration or let a dead rubber drift.

The Real Decider ⚽ Irankunda · Watford

Paraguay v Australia

While the USA party next door, this is the game in Group D that actually matters: a straight shootout for second place and a ticket to the knockouts.

Paraguay 33% Draw 42% Australia 25%
Win probability · market-implied consensus

Here is the genuine drama of the evening, just up the coast from the SoFi festivities. Paraguay and Australia both sit on three points, both chasing the second qualifying spot behind the runaway USA, and the math is taut: Australia hold the edge on goal difference, which means Paraguay likely need a win, while the Socceroos can advance with a draw but dare not assume it. One of these sides goes to the round of 32, the other goes home, and ninety minutes will decide which.

Australia have been the group's great overachievers, stunning Türkiye in their opener thanks to teenage sensation Nestory Irankunda before a narrow loss to the USA. They are organized, hard-running and dangerous on the break, exactly the kind of disciplined side that knows how to grind out the result it needs. A draw sends them through, but they will be wary of sitting too deep and inviting the pressure that can undo a cautious team late.

Paraguay, by contrast, must win, and a side playing with that clarity of purpose can be dangerous. South American steel meets Australian grit in a true knockout-in-disguise, the kind of tense, scrappy, nervy occasion that the final round of group games throws up every four years. It will not be pretty, but it will be gripping, and for one of these teams it ends in heartbreak. Keep it on the second screen all evening.

Upset Watch

Hard to call an upset when the teams are this level. The drama is the tightrope: Australia's draw-is-enough caution against Paraguay's must-win urgency. One goal, or one nervy mistake, likely decides who survives, and the goal-difference math could come down to the final kick.

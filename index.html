First, What You Missed · Thursday 2 July

On Your Doorstep
Round of 32 · Inglewood (SoFi)
ESP Spain 3–0 Austria AUT
Lamine Yamal scores his first World Cup goal in his LA hometown.

The European champions came to Inglewood and put on a clinic. Spain passed Austria dizzy and ran out comfortable 3-0 winners, and the afternoon had a perfect storybook flourish: teenage superstar Lamine Yamal, who has roots in the LA area, scored his first-ever World Cup goal essentially in his own backyard, then was handed a WWE championship belt afterward because 2026 is a deeply silly and wonderful year. Spain look ominous, they are through to face Portugal in the last 16, and if you were anywhere near SoFi, you witnessed the tournament's shiniest team in full flow. Peak doorstep value.

Survived a Scare Round of 32 · Toronto
POR Portugal2–1Croatia CRO

The Ronaldo-Modrić farewell duel delivered proper drama. Portugal edged a tense, exploding-into-life second half to beat Croatia 2-1, sending Luka Modrić into the international sunset and Cristiano Ronaldo, 41 and utterly unbothered by time, into a last-16 date with neighbors Spain. It was closer than the scoreline suggests and Croatia will feel they had their chances, but the old warriors of Portugal march on. One legend advances, one bows out. Football, being poetic.

Round of 32 · Vancouver
SUI Switzerland3–0Algeria ALG

Switzerland did the most Swiss thing imaginable: quietly, efficiently, and without any fuss whatsoever, they dismantled Algeria 3-0. Breel Embolo tapped in, Dan Ndoye curled a beauty, and the Desert Foxes' tournament of chaos ended with a tidy Swiss shrug. It is the first time in their history that Switzerland have won three matches in a row at a World Cup, which feels like the understated national achievement of a nation that also invented the very quiet watch. They face Colombia or Ghana next.

The Storylines

The Smallest Fairytale

Cabo Verde, a cluster of ten islands off West Africa with roughly half a million people, are the smallest nation ever to reach a World Cup knockout stage, and they did it on debut. They held Spain to a 0-0 draw. Let that sink in. Today they get Messi and the defending champions, and while the odds are cartoonishly against them, the Blue Sharks have already turned the tournament into a bedtime story. If they so much as score, half the planet will cheer. This is what the World Cup is for.

Club Connections

Star power aplenty. Argentina roll out Lionel Messi (Inter Miami) and Lautaro Martínez (Inter Milan); Egypt lean on Mohamed Salah (Liverpool) and Omar Marmoush (Man City). Colombia parade Luis Díaz (Bayern Munich) and James Rodríguez, with Ghana's Antoine Semenyo (Bournemouth) and Mohammed Kudus (Tottenham) carrying the underdog hopes. Australia's Jackson Irvine anchors the Socceroos against the Pharaohs.

Where to Point Your Eyeballs

The whole slate sits in friendly West Coast hours. The unmissable one is the 3pm Argentina v Cabo Verde, not because it will be close, but because watching Messi conduct a masterclass while a debutant nation lives its wildest dream is the good stuff. Start early with the 11am Egypt v Australia, a genuinely tight affair with Salah involved, and close out with Colombia v Ghana at 6:30pm. A gentle, goal-friendly Friday. Fire up the group chat.

Record Watch · The Golden Boot

Lionel Messi arrives on six goals, level at the top of the scoring charts with Kylian Mbappé, and a Cabo Verde side that has conceded sparingly but faces a very different tier of attack today. If Messi is in the mood, he could nudge ahead in the Golden Boot race, and given this is almost certainly his final World Cup, every goal now carries the weight of a farewell. Keep an eye on the Argentina scoreline; history tends to follow this man around.

Neither of these teams was supposed to be here, which makes it all the more fun that one of them is about to reach the last 16. Egypt are marginal favorites, and the reason is spelled S-A-L-A-H: Mohamed Salah remains one of the best forwards on the planet, and alongside Manchester City's Omar Marmoush he gives the Pharaohs a genuine cutting edge that dragged them through a group containing Belgium and Iran. When Egypt click, they have the individual quality to beat anyone, and they will back themselves to find a moment against a workmanlike Australia.

The Socceroos, for their part, are exactly what you expect from an Australian team: organized, relentless, physically imposing, and utterly allergic to giving up. They ground their way out of a tough group through sheer bloody-mindedness, and while they lack a match-winner of Salah's class, they compensate with cohesion and a refusal to be bullied. Their game plan will be simple: frustrate Egypt, smother Salah, keep it tight, and try to nick something from a set piece or a scrappy moment. It is not glamorous, but it works more often than the neutrals expect.

This projects as a cagey, tense, low-scoring affair, the kind of match that gets settled by a single flash of Salah brilliance or one gritty Australian sucker-punch. Egypt have the higher ceiling and the better player, so they are rightly favored, but the gap is narrow and the Socceroos are precisely the sort of stubborn opponent who can drag a favorite into extra time and beyond. A tight one to warm up the day, with a real chance of drama late.

Upset Watch

Very live, if you can even call it an upset. Australia are only slim underdogs, and their organized, physical approach is tailor-made to neutralize a Salah-dependent Egypt. If they smother the Liverpool man and force a scrappy game, the Socceroos are more than capable of grinding out a win or dragging this to penalties.

The defending champions and the greatest of all time against the smallest nation ever to reach a World Cup knockout stage. A mismatch on paper, a fairytale in the flesh.

Let us be honest about the football first: this should be a comfortable Argentina win. The defending champions have looked ruthless, winning all three group games and going 8-1 on aggregate, with Lionel Messi rolling back the years to score six goals and sit level atop the Golden Boot race. Lautaro Martínez and Julián Álvarez give Scaloni multiple routes to goal, the defense has barely been troubled, and this is a team that knows exactly how to win a knockout game without breaking a sweat. On pure quality, it is a mismatch of almost comic proportions.

But football is not played on paper, and Cabo Verde have already written one of the great World Cup stories. An archipelago of roughly half a million people, competing in their first ever World Cup, they reached the knockouts by being maddeningly hard to break down, holding a rotated Spain to a goalless draw and drawing with Uruguay along the way. They will do here what they have done all tournament: sit deep, defend in numbers, compress the space, and dare a great team to pick the lock. It worked against Spain. Whether it works against a locked-in Messi is the question.

The overwhelming likelihood is that Argentina's patience and quality eventually tell, most probably in a controlled, measured win where Messi conjures a moment or two and the champions ease through. But there is a reason to watch beyond the result: this is a nation of half a million living a dream nobody thought possible, sharing a pitch with the greatest player of all time in what is surely his final World Cup. If Cabo Verde score, the neutral world will erupt. If they somehow hold on, it would be the shock of the century. Either way, savor it.

Upset Watch

Faint, but fairytales do not care about odds. Cabo Verde's whole identity is frustrating superior teams, and they already blanked Spain. It would take a perfect defensive night, a heroic goalkeeping display, and a rare off-day from Messi, but the Blue Sharks have spent this whole tournament doing things nobody believed they could.

Colombia are quietly one of the more intriguing teams left in the draw. They topped a tough group by holding Portugal to a goalless draw and generally looking organized, dangerous and hard to beat, and the bracket has fallen kindly enough that they can genuinely dream of a deep run. Luis Díaz is their star turn, a Bayern Munich winger with the pace and directness to unlock any defense, and with the veteran guile of James Rodríguez pulling strings behind him, Los Cafeteros have both flair and control. They arrive as clear favorites and know it.

Ghana, by contrast, lived dangerously just to get here, sneaking through as one of the best third-placed teams after a group campaign that flickered rather than blazed. The Black Stars have talent, Bournemouth's Antoine Semenyo and Tottenham's Mohammed Kudus can hurt anyone on their day, but they have been inconsistent, and their star men have been quieter than Ghana would like. To pull off the upset, they will need Semenyo to produce the game of his life and Kudus to finally catch fire, and they will need to withstand plenty of Colombian pressure in the process.

The likeliest outcome is a Colombian win, though Ghana have the individual quality to make it a nervy, competitive night if their big names show up. Expect Colombia to control possession and territory, expect Díaz to be a constant menace, and expect Ghana to threaten in bursts on the counter. Some predict extra time; the smart read is a Colombian edge with a real chance of late drama. A fitting, watchable close to a Round of 32 that has delivered chaos, fairytales and giant-killings in equal measure.

Upset Watch

Possible if Ghana's stars wake up. Semenyo and Kudus have the talent to swing any game, and if they click on the counter, Colombia's deep-run dream could wobble. But Ghana have been inconsistent all tournament, and Colombia are the more balanced, in-form side. The Black Stars need their big names, and fast.
